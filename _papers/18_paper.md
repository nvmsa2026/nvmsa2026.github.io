**Abstract**: Solid-state transformer (SST) offers compact conversion, galvanic isolation, and advanced controllability by replacing line-frequency transformers with high-frequency power-electronic stages. As modular SST platforms scale to higher power and higher availability, however, health monitoring, fault diagnosis, and control must compete for limited computation and communication resources under stringent timing constraints. This paper presents a co-designed real-time monitoring and scheduling framework for a modular SST configured as a 13-kV AC to 800-V DC multi-module rectifier chain. The proposed architecture integrates thread-per-phase data acquisition, multi-process isolation, priority-aware task queues, deadline-aware filtering, and load-shedding mechanisms to preserve timing predictability while maintaining diagnostic integrity. Simulation studies are conducted for 10-, 20-, and 30-module configurations, and performance is evaluated in terms of end-to-end latency, jitter, CPU utilization, memory footprint, context-switch overhead, and false-positive/false-negative behavior. The results indicate that the framework remains stable and diagnostically reliable at the 10- and 20-module scales, whereas the 30-module case approaches a practical real-time boundary because of synchronization overhead and burst-event contention. The analysis further identifies CPU affinity, lock-free shared-memory queues, interrupt coalescing, and zero-copy communication as effective engineering directions for extending scalability.
