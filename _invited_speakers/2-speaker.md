**Abstract**: The abundance of system-level optimizations for extending SSD lifetime stands in stark contrast to the lack of theoretical algorithmic results, or even interest, in this problem domain. This talk will follow our interdisciplinary effort to bridge this gap between the theory and practice of SSD optimization. We started by introducing the first formal definition of SSD management as an optimization problem. We then explored this problem from an algorithmic perspective, considering both offline and online settings, and bound the worst-case performance of several existing and new algorithms.  Our formal problem definition formed a new model that we later used to analyze SSD wear leveling, PCM wear leveling under adversarial attacks, and SSD-based caches.

In this talk, I will explore the challenges of introducing a new model: identifying the right level of abstraction, deciding which objectives to optimize, comparing algorithmic worst-case results to the state-of-the-art systems, and convincing two very different research communities that the model the model is both important and useful.

**Bio**: Gala Yadgar is an Associate Professor in the Henry and Marilyn Taub Faculty of Computer Science at the Technion – Israel Institute of Technology. Her research focuses on storage systems for large-scale data centers, spanning storage hierarchies, flash-based storage, caching, deduplication, erasure coding, and edge storage systems. Prof. Yadgar currently serves as Program Co-Chair of the 2026 ACM SIGOPS Annual Technical Conference (ATC), previously co-chaired USENIX FAST 2021, HotStorage 2019, and ACM SYSTOR 2018, and is an Associate Editor of ACM Transactions on Storage (TOS).

Her research has appeared in the field's leading venues, including USENIX FAST, USENIX ATC, ICDCS, ACM Transactions on Storage (TOS), ACM Transactions on Computer Systems (TOCS), (POMACS/SIGMETRICS), and Communications of the ACM. Her work described in this keynote was recognized by research highlights in Communications of the ACM and a SIGMETRICS best student paper award.
